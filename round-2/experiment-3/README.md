## Layman Summary

We retuned two training methods on a small image-recognition task and checked whether the sharpness-aware one gives more trustworthy confidence scores; its advantage holds only for the probability-scoring measure, not every calibration measure.

## Full Summary

This artifact compares sharpness-aware minimization (SAM) with plain SGD for a small two-block convolutional network trained on Fashion-MNIST. Learning rates and the SAM neighbourhood size rho are chosen on a validation split only, using validation NLL, with learning rates tested below the old grid edge; the selected settings are SGD lr 0.001 and SAM lr 0.01 with rho 0.1. Five paired seeds are trained per arm, and each final model is evaluated on the official 10k test set. Reported metrics are accuracy, expected calibration error (ECE, 15 equal-width bins), negative log-likelihood (NLL), the multi-class Brier score, and a top-label equal-count adaptive calibration error (ACE). Temperature scaling fitted on validation gives a temperature-scaled SGD control and a temperature-scaled SAM row. The headline is a 12-row paired family with paired 95% t-intervals and Holm correction. SAM improves accuracy by about 1 point (a bound, not an equivalence), and its NLL is lower than temperature-scaled SGD by about 0.033 (interval excludes zero, Holm p = 0.0018). After temperature scaling, the ECE and ACE differences are not significant. Temperatures show raw SGD is over-confident and raw SAM is under-confident. Final training loss is reported beside every calibration row. The shuffled-label placebo is significant for NLL and Brier, so it does not isolate label information for confidence-sensitive metrics; this is reported as a limitation. Results apply to this dataset and this small architecture only. Outputs include method.py, the full, mini and preview method-output JSON files, tuning and analysis tables, reliability bins, logits, an independent re-derivation script, and a reproducibility guide.
